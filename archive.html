The Show

A tale of tragedy, kindness, and a lot of casseroles.

How often do you hear an engaging story where people behave with generosity and kindness, coming together to support a family in need?

Suburban Tribe is a theatrical "thank you" to the New Jersey community that rallied around Kate Mura's family after her father's freak accident. Told through physical storytelling, corporeal mime, and improv, it shifts from tragedy to acceptance by way of the race track, a few ghosts, and lots and lots of casseroles.

"In 50 minutes, she switches voices and characterizations effortlessly, and offers vivid representations of people who exhibit compassion in a trying time. It gives you hope that people can be like this all the time, not just in the wake of tragedy."

Theater Jones

She performed (or rather took the form of) eight different characters from the community, each taking a turn telling the story from their own point of view, according to The Penning Girl.

How the Story Began

Ok fine, they were ripped off by three thugs in Paris.

Suburban Tribe began as a short mask show and grew into a full-length piece performed nationally and internationally from 2013 to 2015. Flying home from a successful run at the Avignon OFF Festival, Kate's masks, costume, money, and family heirlooms were stolen at the airport.

"It's kind of strange to be doing a show I've spent hundreds of hours on, performed nationally and internationally, unmasked. Until now, it's been a mask show. Now I'm taking the mask off."

As resiliency is one of the show's core themes, Kate had to rebuild, continuing, in real time, the story of transforming tragedy into creation. Suburban Tribe: Unmasked revisited the work without masks: "It feels like new water. Like skinny dipping for the first time. The cool exhilaration of having no barriers. Perfect freedom. Then wondering: why did I ever wear a bathing suit? Or in my case, a mask."

That instinct to keep peeling the story back kept going. Over the following decade, the mask show became a fully-scripted, multi-character solo play, and found its current name: The Play About My Father.

The Artist

Transformation is my passion…

Kate Mura is an actor, a union stagehand, designer, and coach. She chooses theatre as her life's work because it constantly proves that creating other worlds is possible, and it's her quest, in life and art, to help build the world she wants to live in: one that's beautiful, supportive, and sustainable through tragedy and pain.

  • Backup dancer for Salt-N-Pepa at CityKids Foundation's Tenth Anniversary Gala
  • Danced with President Bill Clinton at the Presidential Summit for America's Future
  • Coached William Hurt in neutral mask for No Man's Land at Artists Repertory Theatre
  • Played The Library Lady in Tomás and the Library Lady with Oregon Children's Theatre
  • Toured ten states with Tears of Joy Theatre in The Reluctant Dragon, Pinocchio, and When Animals Were People
  • Recipient of the Leslie O. Fulton Fellowship

When she's not in a theatre, she can be found exploring her surroundings, reading tarot cards, and being naked in nature.

Producing Company

Fuse Theatre Ensemble

Fuse has spent over a decade producing paradigm-shifting theatre in Portland, OR, and around the world, a recipient of six 2019 Drammy Awards and grants from the Regional Arts & Culture Council. In under a decade, Fuse fostered thirteen original works and toured the Pacific Northwest's work to New Orleans, Dallas, Albuquerque, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Bali, Adelaide, Bath, and Avignon, while also producing Portland's annual OUTwright Theatre Festival.
